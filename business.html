Data Pipeline: From Zero to Production

Situation

A large e-commerce retailer's order data was consumed by multiple business domains. A legacy pipeline existed but had unclear ownership, and the project to rebuild it had been halted.

Stock Claim Investigation

Uncovered a hidden process issue and implemented a solution to recover operational capacity

Click to expand
Situation

Stock claims were accumulating due to orders stuck in an undefined status. The root cause was unknown, the scope was unclear, and the issue was affecting multiple stakeholders.

Task

Investigate the issue independently, identify the root cause, quantify the impact, and deliver a solution.

Action

First, conducted a cross-functional investigation, gathering insights from multiple teams to piece together the full picture. Identified that orders which couldn't be fulfilled were later holding stock claims, taking up storage space and consuming purchasing capacity, even after refunds had been processed.

Second, designed and implemented an automated process to remove known stock claims. Performed statistical analysis to quantify the impact, establishing confidence intervals for cost recovery.

Result

Root cause identified and documented
Automated stock claim removal flag implemented
Statistical analysis confirmed measurable cost recovery
Purchasing capacity improved

Real Car Collision Avoidance

Successfully tested automated emergency maneuvers on a real electric vehicle in Japan

Click to expand
Situation

Automated emergency braking and steering systems often fail in sudden obstacle scenarios because they're too slow or too conservative. Testing these systems on real cars (not just simulations) is expensive, risky, and rarely done. I had the opportunity to work on this at e-Mobility & Control lab's electric vehicle lab.

Task

Implement and test a collision avoidance system on a real electric vehicle. The goal was to prove that an intelligent control system can react faster and more safely than human drivers in "moose test" (sudden static obstacles on the road) scenarios .

Action

On-the-fly adaptation: Upon starting the assignment, I discovered that the platform differed significantly from what we had assumed in the proposal. I rewrote the implementation plan on the spot to work with the actual hardware and sensors available.

Design & testing: Developed a dual-planner system combining predictive control with a human-like "maximum steering" fallback. Tested it across multiple speeds and hazard levels on a real test track and validated the system's ability to avoid collisions where the controller alone failed.

H4MPC: A Toolbox for Automated Driving

Open-source MATLAB toolbox that reduces computational complexity in real-time control systems

Click to expand
Situation

Real-time control systems in automated driving face a fundamental trade-off: accuracy vs. computational speed. Nonlinear control algorithms deliver better performance but are too slow for real-time applications, which is a critical barrier to commercialization.

Task

Design a practical solution that makes nonlinear control computationally tractable without sacrificing performance that is accessible to engineers and academics.

Action

Technical delivery: Developed H4MPC, an open-source MATLAB toolbox with a graphical user interface that automates the process of approximating complex nonlinear functions with computationally efficient alternatives.

Verification: Built two real-world case studies to validate performance: vehicle dynamics and tire saturation constraints.

Result

Presented toolbox at IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium, Kyoto, Japan
Open-source release with active adoption
Demonstrated real-time viability in a peer-reviewed publication
Bridged the applicability gap between academic research and commercial incorporation

Proactive Emergency Collision Avoidance

Stochastic control strategy that enables safer, less conservative emergency maneuvers in highway scenarios

Click to expand
Situation

Automated driving systems often take overly conservative decisions in emergency situations. For example, they take unnecessary braking because they can't predict other drivers' behavior. This creates safety risks (e.g., rear-end collisions) and reduces trust in autonomous systems.

Task

Develop a control strategy that enables proactive emergency maneuvers (such as safely changing lanes to avoid a collision) rather than just reacting with conservative braking.

Action

Technical delivery: Designed a stochastic predictive planner that accounts for uncertainty in other vehicles' behavior. The design uses advanced approximation techniques to enable real-time implementation.

Verification: Validated the approach in industry standard high-fidelity simulations and benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods.
